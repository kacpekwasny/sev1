# Route server — the neighborhood gossip

## The full-mesh problem

If every host has a [[bgp-podstawy|BGP]] session with every other host, session count
grows quadratically: `n(n-1)/2`.

| Hosts | Sessions |
|---|---|
| 10 | 45 |
| 100 | 4,950 |
| 1000 | 499,500 |

A thousand hosts means half a million TCP sessions to maintain and reestablish
after each restart. That does not scale.

## The solution: one gossip

Everyone talks only to the **route server**. They tell it what they know, and it
passes the information on. There are `n` sessions instead of `n(n-1)/2`.

```
   host1 ─┐        ┌─ host3
          ├─ RS ───┤
   host2 ─┘        └─ host4
```

Like a neighborhood gossip: you do not need to know everyone, just tell the right person.

## How it differs from an ordinary router

A route server **does not forward traffic**. It sits beside the data path and
only distributes information. It therefore usually preserves the original
`next-hop` and does not add itself to `as-path`. Our route servers most often
use [[frr-i-gobgp|GoBGP]], with routes generated by our own service.

## The tradeoff

Everything passes through one point, so we deploy at least two and keep host
sessions to both. We also have to be careful about who advertises what to whom:
a bad route-server policy spreads across the whole network immediately.

# The internship interview — honestly

## What we look for

- **Interest and effort** — you built something because it interested you, not just for a grade.
- **Resourcefulness** — “I don't know” is followed by “but here is how I would check.”
- **Persistence** — a problem you worked on for three days says more than a technology list.
- **A good teammate** — someone to talk and joke with, then work together until 11 p.m. on an [[sev1-incydent|incident]].

We are not looking for slick bluffing. A stretched answer falls apart on the
second question.

## What we do not test

Memorizing every flag, lecture definition, or algorithm we never implement.
Documentation is open at work too.

## How a technical interview works

You get a problem slightly too large for the allotted time. We want to see
**how you think**:

- Do you clarify assumptions before starting?
- Can you break the problem into pieces?
- Do you accept hints and use them?
- Can you see what might go wrong?

## Example questions

1. On a host, `ping 8.8.8.8` works but `curl https://example.com` does not. What next?
2. A service works well but pauses for a second once a day. Where do you start?
3. You have a million routes and hardware capacity for 32,000; see [[zadania/fib-nie-miesci-sie|this exercise]].
4. Tell us about something you broke. Seriously — it is a good question.

## Practical advice

Have one project you can discuss for ten minutes: what it does, why you built it
that way, and what you would change today. That works better than a list of courses.
